Preparing for Your Cardiology Appointment

A little preparation makes your cardiology visit more productive. The more complete the picture of your health you bring, the better we can plan care together. You don't need to be an expert — you just need the right pieces of information.

On this page

  • What to bring
  • Organizing your medications
  • Questions to ask
  • What to expect
  • If you need tests or reports

What to Bring

  • Photo ID and your insurance card.
  • A list of your medications (see below).
  • Recent test results and reports — ECG, stress tests, echocardiogram, labs, or imaging from other providers, if you have them.
  • Your blood pressure readings if you monitor at home — bring the log if you keep one.
  • Relevant medical records or a summary from doctors you've seen for heart issues.
  • Name and contact of your primary care doctor and other specialists.
  • A family member or friend if you'd like help listening and remembering — very helpful on a first or complex visit.
  • A list of your questions written down (see template below).

Organizing Your Medications

Bring your most current medication list with the name, dose, and how often you take each one — including low-dose aspirin, over-the-counter medicines, vitamins, and supplements. Or simply bring all your pill bottles in a bag. This lets us review everything you take, which matters for heart patients (interactions and dose adjustments are common).

If you're unsure about a medicine, a pill box or the pill bottles themselves work perfectly — we'll sort it out together.

Questions to Ask

It helps to bring a few written questions. Good starting points:

  • What is my current diagnosis, and what does it mean for me day to day?
  • What are my blood pressure, cholesterol, and other key numbers?
  • Do I need any tests, and what will they tell us?
  • Are my current medications right for me, or is there something better?
  • What can I do about my symptoms (chest discomfort, shortness of breath, palpitations, fatigue)?
  • What activity, exercise, or diet should I change?
  • When should I call the office, and when should I call 911?

What to Expect

  • Medication and history review — we'll talk through your health history, symptoms, and lifestyle.
  • Physical exam — heart, lungs, pulse, and checking for fluid retention (ankles, belly), plus blood pressure.
  • Possible tests — an ECG is often done in the office. You may be offered a stress test, echo, wearable monitor, or labs if helpful.
  • A plan you understand — at the end, you should know what the next steps are, what medications if any, and when to follow up.

It's completely fine to ask us to slow down or repeat something. This is your visit.

If You Need Tests or Reports

If we order tests, we'll tell you how to prepare — for example, how long to fast before labs or hold certain medications before a stress test. If you need records from another hospital, let us know and we'll help coordinate. (See our procedures pages for how to prepare for a stress test, cardiac catheterization, or other tests.)

Key Takeaways

  • Bring ID, insurance, your medication list, recent test results, and your blood pressure log.
  • Write down your questions ahead of time and bring a support person if helpful.
  • Expect a history review, exam, and possibly an ECG or other tests.
  • Leave knowing your plan, your numbers, and what to watch for.
